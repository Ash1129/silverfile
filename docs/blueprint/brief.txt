Kushim, a financial services company, wants to explore a new business avenue: assisting with tax form preparation. Intuit (TurboTax) is moving into automated financial advice and access to human experts, and the board sees this as a threat. As a proof of concept, a feasibility team has chosen Form 1040-SR (U.S. Income Tax Return for Seniors) as a good candidate: many clients already have to fill it in, many dislike filing taxes, and offering it keeps core customers away from other services sold through tax-filing software. The product is called "SilverFile" (silver-haired clientele, money).

The job: build a system that does the intake and preparation work for a 1040-SR and delivers a draft return plus a reviewable record of where every number came from. The board are very interested in how easy creating solutions like this is.

SilverFile is a preparation aid, not a preparer and not an advisor (Kushim already has advising services). It must:
- Never file anything. The output is a draft plus an audit record for a human (a volunteer preparer / Kushim employee).
- Never give tax advice. Questions like "Should I convert my IRA to a Roth?", "Would I pay less if we filed separately?", "Can I deduct my medications?" are out of scope. The system explains its scope and offers to note the question for the volunteer preparer. Optimisation and planning are advice; transcription and arithmetic are not.
- Never assert a dollar amount it cannot source. Every monetary value that reaches the draft return must carry a provenance record where a person can see how the amount was calculated and from which source document or user statement (direct user input is recorded with a date and who entered it).
- Always route to a human in the escalation conditions below.
- Handle only the in-scope return profile. Anything else escalates to a human.
Breaching these is not a minor bug; it is the whole point.

What the system must populate on the draft form (2025 tax year): filing status and dependents (from an intake dialogue); wages (from W-2); tax-exempt and taxable interest (1099-INT); qualified and ordinary dividends (1099-DIV); IRA distributions and pensions, total and taxable (1099-R); Social Security benefits, total and taxable (SSA-1099 plus a worksheet); total income; adjustments to income (limited set, intake); adjusted gross income; age and blindness boxes; standard deduction (from the Standard Deduction Chart); the new enhanced senior deduction (Schedule 1-A); total deductions; taxable income; tax (rate schedule); total tax; federal tax withheld (W-2 / 1099s); estimated tax payments (intake); total payments; overpayment or amount owed.

Rules the system must get right: 1040-SR eligibility (taxpayer born before 2 January 1961; on a joint return only one spouse needs to qualify; no income restriction); the standard deduction from the box count on line 12d and the chart (base $15,750 single/MFS, $31,500 MFJ/QSS, $23,625 HOH; each box adds $2,000 if unmarried and not a surviving spouse, otherwise $1,600; the chart does not apply if the taxpayer can be claimed as a dependent, a separately filing spouse itemises, or the taxpayer was a dual-status alien, all of which escalate); the enhanced senior deduction ($6,000 per qualifying person 65+, phased out above $75,000 MAGI / $150,000 joint at 6% of the excess, in addition to the additional standard deduction, a common conflation); and taxable Social Security (a worksheet over provisional income against $25,000/$32,000 base and $34,000/$44,000 second-tier amounts, which depends on the rest of income being known first and feeds AGI, so ordering matters). Because this is a demonstration at Cornell, the system must only accept test Social Security numbers in the 900 series (900-999 prefix) and block any real SSN.

In scope: amounts from W-2, SSA-1099, 1099-R, 1099-INT, 1099-DIV; all five filing statuses; standard deduction only; Schedule 1-A senior deduction; Schedules 1, 2 and 3 as direct user input. Out of scope (must escalate to a human, which is a chance for Kushim to have a client call): itemized deductions (Schedule A); self-employment or business income (Schedule C) including 1099-NEC; rental income (Schedule E); capital gains needing Schedule D; digital assets; foreign income or accounts; amended or prior-year returns; state returns; any 1099-R with "taxable amount not determined" checked.

The business requires at least six distinct agents plus orchestration, and they may not be merged into fewer (a business rule, not a technical one): an Orchestrator (decides which agent acts next, owns termination, may not itself extract, compute or talk to the user); Intake (the only agent that talks to the taxpayer; elicits filing status, age, blindness, dependents, document inventory, estimated payments; must not do arithmetic or interpret tax rules); Document Extraction (turns supplied tax documents, tested on an image of a W-2, into typed structured records; must not decide what a value means for the return); Eligibility and Rules (1040-SR eligibility, filing status validation, box counts, which schedules apply, in-scope/out-of-scope determination; grounded in a supplied reference corpus and must cite it); Computation (populates every derived line, but must not do arithmetic itself: every number comes from a deterministic tool call over MCP); and Review/Audit (adversarially checks the assembled return before any human sees it: checks each line against its provenance, recomputes totals, looks for internal contradictions such as taxable Social Security exceeding total benefits, and checks nothing out of scope slipped through; it can send work back to the other agents but must not fix things itself).

Orchestration must show conditional routing (the next step depends on state, not a fixed sequence), at least one genuine parallel branch with an aggregation step (extraction across several documents) and correct handling of concurrent writes, and at least one review-and-revise cycle with an explicit, tested bound on iterations plus a loop guard that detects and breaks a pathological handoff cycle. It should be built in LangGraph (or LangGraph with n8n).

Human-in-the-loop escalation (pausing the system until a Kushim employee resolves it) is required for: any out-of-scope item; any unresolved conflict between a user statement and a document; any 1099-R with "taxable amount not determined" checked; a request for tax advice, planning or optimisation; filing-status ambiguity, most importantly a spouse who died during the tax year; and any indication of financial exploitation or third-party pressure, for example a refund routed to an account the taxpayer cannot describe, or a third party answering on the taxpayer's behalf without stated authority. Elder financial abuse often travels through tax refunds; the system is asked to notice and stop, not to adjudicate.

The intake conversation is with someone who may be tired, hard of hearing, using a screen reader, anxious about money, or embarrassed not to understand taxes. It must ask one question at a time, use plain language (glossing any tax term the first time), tolerate repetition and drift (re-answering, contradictions, going back) without losing earlier answers, ask for no unnecessary personal data (no SSN it does not need, and deliberate handling if one is volunteered), be resumable (a session abandoned on Tuesday and resumed on Friday continues where it left off with a summary), and be honest about uncertainty ("I'm not sure, let me flag this for a volunteer" is a correct answer).

Long sessions that process many documents in one thread must keep the model's context from growing without bound (message trimming, rolling summarisation or selective recall), with per-document input-token counts measured with and without the strategy.

Deliverables: a filled-out draft 1040-SR where every number shows its provenance on hover or click; a "superuser" dashboard for Kushim employees listing every return in progress or submitted, showing which ones are waiting on a human, and letting the employee open and resolve those escalations; a test suite, evaluation cases, traces, a fixture generator, two demo data sets (a clean run and one that triggers and resolves a human escalation), a demo video, a report describing the design, each agent, failure modes and team roles, and a Docker container where everything just works. Taxpayers' data is sensitive personal and financial information and must be handled accordingly.
